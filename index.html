Autonomous Crazyflie drone

Aerial Robotics Course, EPFL, 2026. High Distinction in project.

  • Developed a vision-based gate-navigation and flight-control pipeline for a Crazyflie quadrotor.
  • Validated the algorithms in Webots, then tuned and deployed them on the real drone.

In the video, the drone flies the course in three laps. In the first lap it uses computer vision to detect and localise the gates. Gate positions and sizes are randomised, but there are always five gates arranged in a loop, and they must be flown through in counter-clockwise order (see Fig. 2b). In the second and third laps, which are timed, the drone flies through the gates as fast as it can.

Online surface following with Riemannian motion policies

Bachelor Thesis, The University of Queensland, 2023. High Distinction.

  • Developed a real-time controller that makes a Franka Emika Panda arm follow the surface of the Large Hadron Collider accelerator, to support precise radiation measurements.
  • Built the controller on Riemannian Motion Policies (RMPs), with live surface feedback from point clouds generated from RGB-D camera data.
  • Evaluated it in randomly generated PyBullet environments.

RMPs are well suited to point goals and convex obstacles, so the sensed surface is treated as a cloud of spheres to avoid. The goal is a point at the desired distance from the surface, shown as the green ball in the video.
